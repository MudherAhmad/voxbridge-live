# VoxBridge Live design

The studio opens directly to the Swedish-to-Arabic workflow. The studio title, mode, status, and playback controls share one toolbar, so the timeline begins directly beneath it. The browser tests assert vertical placement on desktop and mobile. Desktop has two synchronized vertical audio lanes followed by two parallel caption panes; narrow screens stack the panes. Amber labels Swedish and mint labels Arabic, with language names as additional cues. Highlight one workshop term per segment. Workshop action extraction is out of the current UI scope.

The bundled sample is deliberately labeled. Two independent pitch-preserving media elements handle per-language speeds. One transport line crosses both lanes. The transport follows the source up to the Arabic cue and follows the dub through the rest of the phrase. The player advances by sentence pair and pauses both media elements to preserve their positions for resume. The sample plays each source phrase to completion before its dub. Source ducking remains in live capture. Each track has an alternate separately synthesized woman’s voice and three pitch and tone treatments. All voices have matching cue boundaries and their own measured waveforms. Wave peaks are taken from those WAVs. The clip is independent of browser voices, API keys, and the production platform.

Capture and sample sessions are mutually exclusive. Capture owns the display tracks and releases them at stop. When the browser reports source suppression, the capture track is routed through a gain stage for live ducking. Otherwise the browser keeps control of original playback.

Live dubbing starts after a separate user action. The browser receives the captured track for Swedish recognition, with provider-dependent online processing. Final speech segments go to the browser Translator API and appear as paired live captions. A FIFO browser synthesis queue runs one Arabic utterance at a time and accelerates playback to 1.25× with a backlog. Switching to captions or stopping sharing cancels active and pending speech. Three translated pairs are retained transiently. A contextual translation or neural voice provider is a future alternative.
