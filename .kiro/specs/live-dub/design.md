# VoxBridge Live design

The studio opens directly to the Swedish-to-Arabic workflow. The studio title, mode, status, and playback controls share one toolbar, so the timeline begins directly beneath it. The browser tests assert vertical placement on desktop and mobile. Desktop has two synchronized vertical audio lanes followed by two parallel caption panes; narrow screens stack the panes. Amber labels Swedish and mint labels Arabic, with language names as additional cues. Highlight one workshop term per segment. Workshop action extraction is out of the current UI scope.

The bundled sample is labeled. Two independently adjustable, pitch-preserving media elements share a playhead. The player advances by sentence pair, completes the source before its dub, and preserves positions on pause. Swedish offers the original, an alternate woman’s voice, and a youthful treatment. Arabic adds deep and mature treatments. The packaged tracks have matching cue boundaries and wave peaks measured from their WAV files. Sample playback uses the packaged tracks on each supported device.

Capture and sample sessions are mutually exclusive. Capture owns the display tracks and releases them at stop. When the browser reports source suppression, the capture track is routed through a gain stage for live ducking. Otherwise the browser keeps control of original playback.

Live dubbing starts after a separate user action. The browser receives the captured track for Swedish recognition, with provider-dependent online processing. Final phrases go to the browser Translator API and appear as paired live captions. A FIFO browser synthesis queue runs one Arabic utterance at a time and accelerates playback to 1.25× with a backlog. Native speech start and end events drive the Arabic activity meter and source attenuation. Switching to captions or stopping sharing cancels active and pending speech. Three translated pairs are retained transiently for captions. If Arabic synthesis is unavailable, the lane identifies the missing voice, captions continue, and the studio provides a recovery instruction. A contextual translation or neural voice provider is future work.

Interaction references: [Linear](https://linear.app/) for hierarchy, [Descript](https://www.descript.com/) for pairing text and audio, and [Fairlight](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve/fairlight) for linked tracks.
